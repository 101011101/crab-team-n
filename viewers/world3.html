Layering covers more floor

Measured inside the joint model, so all four rows share one scale. Floor area is counted as occupied cells after projecting onto the solved floor plane.

The three passes walked overlapping but different ground: their camera centroids sit 0.59, 1.02 and 1.56 path-radii apart. That is exactly why the union beats every part — enough overlap to register, enough separation to add.

What this is, and what it is not

  • 13 camera poses were dropped. At the end of IMG_7375 the camera is pushed up against a cardboard box and a single flat face fills the frame. A plane constrains no depth, so those poses run away — the last three jump 32 model units in one step. They are excluded from the view and every number here.
  • There is no metre anywhere on this page. Monocular SfM cannot recover scale. Distances are model units. A tag of known printed size, or a measured baseline, is the only fix.
  • The solved focal is not calibration. The free bundle returned 1682.7 px for a 1080×1920 frame, a 59.4° field across the long side. That is in the right region for an iPhone 13 wide camera in video crop, which is a plausibility check and not a measurement. This repo has twice been burned trusting a solved focal; calibrate the camera instead.
  • These are triangulated sparse points, not a splat and not a mesh. 61,000 solved points, of which 25,831 are drawn here after keeping only those seen in three or more views and inside five path-radii of the cameras.
  • A person walks through IMG_7373. Moving objects are not modelled; they contribute nothing and are rejected as outliers.
  • One shared camera made this easy. All three clips came off the same iPhone 13 within 68 seconds, so one calibration covers all of them. The repo's own merge study measured the alternative: independent intrinsics gave 65% scale error, a shared focal gave 0.5%.
