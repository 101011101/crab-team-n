# Pitches

> Source: team chat 2026-09-19. See [pitch-lines.md](pitch-lines.md) for the original wording.

## One-liners

- A world from multiple visions.
- A team of super cheap robots that collaborate, explore, and solve problems.
- Crab Team N. A play on Seal Team 6. The N is the point: add a crab, it takes a branch.
- Ant behaviour, built from toy cars and a laptop.
- No single robot sees the room. Together they map it.

## What we are solving

- Some spaces are too small for large robots.
- Some problems have difficult, non-uniform shapes.
- Search and rescue needs coverage before a person enters.
- Exploration and collection need many cheap sensors, not one expensive one.
- Large robots need training data from real spaces.
- Amazon rainforest biodiversity needs wide, repeated, cheap survey.

## Key features

- Four operators and six facts. Every behaviour derives from them.
- Collaboration is not scripted. It is an unsatisfiable precondition finding a crab that can satisfy it.

- Swarm exploration. Robots cover ground without individual steering.
- One shared world assembled from several cheap cameras.
- Ant recruitment. Robots concentrate on high-biodiversity regions automatically.
- Collaboration. One robot clears a blocked path so another can pass.
- Resilience. Kill a robot and its area is reassigned live.
- Cheap hardware. Toy car kits, one laptop.

## The wow factor

- Three cameras worth about the price of a meal produce one navigable 3D world.
- The robots are never told where they are. The world tells them.
- Nobody scripts the convergence. The swarm decides where to concentrate.
- One robot sacrifices its own progress to open a path for another.
- Nobody wrote "call for help". It falls out of four rules.

## How the wow factor solves the problem

- A single robot cannot map a space it cannot fit through. Multiple views assembled into one world removes that limit.
- Expensive sensors do not scale to wide survey. Cheap cameras do, if the fusion happens off-robot.
- Losing one unit must not lose the mission. Work is held by the shared world, not by the robot.
- The output is a reusable map, so the same run that surveys a space also produces training data for a larger robot.

## Claims we must not make

- We do not have outdoor capability.
- We do not have autonomous charging.
- We do not have decentralised control. One laptop coordinates.
- We do not measure real biodiversity. Coloured markers stand in for species.
